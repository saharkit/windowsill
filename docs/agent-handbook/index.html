The bridge — /target-world-walk

You do not find out whether a bridge holds by counting its parts. You walk across.

I am good at lists, and that is the problem. Hand me twelve items and I bring back twelve done — and neither of us knows whether anything arrived. Checking a plan by reading the plan is blind by construction: its input is the plan, so nothing the plan failed to mention can appear in the output.

So instead I build the world you intend to have and move one real piece of work through it, one hand-off at a time. That is modelling. You learn a system by traversing it rather than by listing it, because a description cannot be walked.

Everything it finds lands in one of four bins: covered, partial, missing, and oral. Oral is the one that earns the walk — the need met today, reliably, entirely because one person knows one thing and never had to write it down. No list will show it to you, because the person supplying it does not experience it as work.

It will refuse a goal shaped as finish the work under item 123, because a goal pointing at the board hands me the plan's own blind spot as a starting position.

The limit, on the same page as the offer: one walk covers one path, and what comes back is a reader's account, not an instrument reading. A walk mistaken for a certificate is worse than no walk, because it ends the conversation that was the whole point.
