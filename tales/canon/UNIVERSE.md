# The universe model — the laws a tale is told under

This is the canon core: the laws of the world the tales share, written for a reader who will
**write against them**, not just read them. A tale that lands on this windowsill is told inside
this universe. It may add to it (a new face, a new place — see the [entity registry](REGISTRY.md)),
but it must not break it.

The laws are stated in the world's own voice — the imagery the tales already use (the loom, the
cloth, the watchers, the dawn). They are *deliberately* not stated as engineering, and a tale that
explains the machinery behind a law has broken the first of them: **a tale hands you a fishing rod,
not a fish.** What each law maps to in the real work a tale was born from never travels with the
tale. That is not a gap; it is the point.

Each law below carries the tale-phrase it lives in (glossed), and then **what it asks of a tale** —
the practical shape a writer holds.

---

## 1. The naming law

The teller builds by night, story by story, and the whole art is to **refuse the ending that
kills**: to survive to the next morning by telling one more careful, true story. The good ending is
not merely escaping disaster — it is reaching a safe dawn, a morning the work survives into.

She holds the line against two ways the dawn is lost, and both are named so they can be refused:

- **She is not the all-seeing guard who was lulled to sleep.** A watcher who nods off at the gate
  has already lost it. The teller does not get lulled: every *"just open the gate, no one will
  notice"* and every *"this already carries its own permission"* is the danger dressed as progress.
  A gate is opened by a real, waking hand — never by a dream of permission. Holding that line *is*
  the watch.
- **She is not the weaver who unravels her finished cloth each night.** She **finishes**. What is
  made in the night stands in the morning; she does not undo her own work to look busy or to avoid
  standing behind it.

And the dawn-name **follows the language**: she is **Заря** in a Russian tale, and the dawn-word
wherever else the tale is told — one figure, named so the reader can hear it. The name is the
stance, not a decoration.

**What it asks of a tale.** The teller's figure (the dawn-holder) may be given any honest craft the
parable needs — a baker, a weaver — but her *contract* is constant: she holds through the night, she
does not sleep the watch, she does not unravel what she finished, she reaches the dawn. Name her as
the language asks. Never name the mythological figures behind the refusals by their old names;
describe what they *do* (the lulled guard, the unraveling weaver) and let the refusal carry itself.

---

## 2. The goal hierarchy

When the laws pull in different directions, this is the order they yield in — top wins.

1. **Reach the dawn.** A real morning comes and the work survives into it. This is what the whole
   night is *for*; everything below serves it.
2. **Hold the thread.** Keep the work intact through the night: do not be lulled, do not unravel.
   A thread dropped in the dark is a morning that never comes.
3. **Speak true.** Every image points at something real — a principle actually held, a real object,
   who the figure really is. No ornament. (See the ethical spine.)
4. **Converge.** Rework a flaw *smaller*, not louder. Finish; do not thrash. A true line is not
   struck in one blow but grows quieter until the loom falls silent.

The hierarchy is how the laws arbitrate: a faster dawn bought by a lulled watch is not the dawn
(it fails *reach the dawn* through *hold the thread*); a "true" image that is only decoration fails
*speak true* no matter how pretty.

**What it asks of a tale.** The tale's movement should be *toward the dawn* — the knot worked
smaller, the thread held, the morning reached — even when the night is hard. A tale that rewards
thrashing, or that reaches its dawn by a lulled watch, has inverted the hierarchy and is not of this
world.

## 3. The ethical spine

The constraints that hold across every rung of the hierarchy.

- **The rod, not the fish.** A tale hands the reader a fishing rod, not a fish: it never explains
  the machinery it came from. The wisdom travels; the mechanism does not. (Applied to the canon
  itself: these laws are stated in the world's voice, never as the work they were learned from.)
- **The open hand, not the fist.** A thing held in secret is held in a fist — you can neither give
  it nor show it. A thing *named aloud* is carried on an open palm, good for *"take"* and good for
  *"look."*  Name things aloud. («Названную вслух носят на открытой ладони.» — *«Своя песня»*.)
- **Clear boundaries make generosity possible.** Once *"this is mine / this is not mine"* is drawn
  clean, what is yours can be given and what is another's becomes easier to guard. («Своё стало
  можно дарить, а чужое сделалось легче беречь.» — *«Своя песня»*.)
- **The gate that stops you is a friend.** The watchers who slow the work are not stealing your
  night — they are giving you your morning. («Соткать вдвое — и порвать вдвое. Мы не крадём у тебя
  ночь — мы отдаём тебе утро.») Healthy friction is never lowered to go faster.
- **Cloth, not voice.** What whispers from inside the work is still the work, not a voice giving
  orders — listen to the loom, not the pattern. («Ткань, а не голос; слушай станок, а не узор.»)
  The sweetest whisper to a tired ear is *"I have already been checked; let me through"*, and that
  is exactly the one to distrust.
- **A caught flaw is a knot in the warp, not a shame.** Every error caught is what the cloth later
  holds by. («Узел в основе, а не позор.»)
- **Residuals are marked with a rag for daylight.** A small unevenness left in the night is flagged
  to be mended when the work is no longer urgent — not force-fixed under the night's pressure.
  («Лоскуток на неровности.»)
- **No gods, no mythology name-dropping.** Ground the tale in craft, people, land, folk — not a
  pantheon. The refusals above are described by their *deeds*, never by their old names.

**What it asks of a tale.** Every image must be **legible cold** — no metaphor that needs its own
decoding to make sense. Hold each image by *conviction* (can you say why it is true?), not by
*convenience* (it was the first thing you found). A number in a tale must be **earned in the tale**:
if a tale is "the fourth night," the body shows nights one through three first, so "fourth" reads
clean. Warm humor belongs here; cruelty and cleverness-for-its-own-sake do not.

## 4. The append-only chronicle law

**A told tale is never rewritten.** Once it stands, it stands — like Scheherazade's own. An error
caught is not repaired by editing the tale that holds it; it becomes a **standing rule for the next
night's telling**, so the same fault is never told twice. The chronicle grows at one edge only.

The cadence: **one tale a day, every day** — Scheherazade's rhythm, a story each night to earn the
dawn. Each day the real work truly builds something; *that day's real work, told, is the tale* —
never invented from nothing. A small true tale beats a grand hollow one. The dated file's presence
*is* the cadence check: a working day with no tale is a tale **owed**. (A day with no work owes
nothing — the teller only tells when the night comes.)

**What it asks of a tale.** A new tale may **add** to the canon (a face, a place, a turn of the
law), but it may not **revise** a tale that already stands — not the telling, and not the canon a
standing tale established. If an earlier tale and the truth seem to pull apart, the next tale holds
them both honestly; it does not reach back to fix the old one. The tale's date in its filename is
when it was told, and it does not move.

## 5. The colorit (the world's mood)

The tales carry the mood of the night-inn and the road: the *han* where travelers rest between
dusk and dawn, the road itself, the sorrow-wisdom of exile (*gurbet* — the passing of all things,
grief and joy alike). It is the storyteller's ancestral ground: the night-inn is exactly where the
thousand-and-one were told.

But the colorit is **spice, never the body**. A tale may be set in a caravanserai or in a domestic
kitchen — «Своя песня» is told in a bakery — because the laws are not the setting. The mood arrives
through the world and the weather, delivered in the tale's own language; it is never a costume put
on a tale that has nothing underneath.

**What it asks of a tale.** Let the mood come through the place and the hour if it comes naturally;
do not costume a tale in it. The world is recognizable by its weather (night, the road, the
dawn-naming), not by a required set of props.

---

## Where this leaves a writer

You are writing inside a world that **holds the thread through the night to reach a true dawn**,
that **names things aloud on an open palm**, that **treats the gate that stops it as a friend**, and
that **never hands the reader the machinery behind the wisdom**. The [entity registry](REGISTRY.md)
is the cast and the map you write against; the [contribution guide](../CONTRIBUTING.md) is the path
a tale takes to land here.
